# Escrow red-team review, 24 October 2026

## Scope

This review covers the EVM escrow (`contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol`) and
the Solana program (`contracts/solana/programs/slop-escrow/src/lib.rs`) in the
open stack #1081 → #1086 → #1091 → #1098 → #1100. It also covers the off-chain
code that builds escrow transactions or decides that a vault is trusted:
both `adapter.ts` files, `contracts/solana/funding.ts`, the owner and Config
planners, and `workers/payment-executor/solana.ts`.

Two independent AI reviewers attacked the #1081 source and the #1091 source.
They proved each finding with a Foundry test or with a transaction on a local
`solana-test-validator`. A third pass reviewed the upgrade code that #1100 adds.
This is an AI review. It is not an independent audit; the owner waived the
audit (PRD section 11).

## Accepted owner policy

These results follow the owner decisions in PRD sections 6, 9 and 11, #1086
and #1100. On 24 October 2026 the owner explicitly accepted the reported
attester-compromise risk and retained immediate binding ([decision](https://github.com/SlopDotCash/slopdotcash/issues/904#issuecomment-6381602244)).
They are accepted authority risks, but every operator must know them.

1. **A stolen identity-authority key can take every matured unpaid award.**
   A binding activates immediately, and #1100 sets the award delay to zero.
   The holder of the identity key binds a wallet that it controls for any actor
   and calls `pay` in the same transaction (EVM: one EIP-7702 batch or two
   transactions in one block; Solana: one transaction). The owner's cancel or
   veto cannot come first. Both reviewers proved this on both chains: an honest
   active binding was replaced and the attacker received 98% of the award.
   With the monthly commitment on, the key can also sign an allocation for an
   actor it controls and take up to the owner's monthly cap each period.
   Protection depends only on custody of the identity key.
2. **The upgrade authority controls all escrowed funds.** #1100 makes the EVM
   factory and every vault UUPS proxies and keeps the Solana program
   upgradeable. The upgrade key can replace the code that holds deposits and
   reserves, with no delay.
3. **Identity-authority rotation has no delay.** The owner's unlimited cancel
   during a pending rotation therefore gives no practical cover.

The README sentence "the owner can cancel in that time" and similar text in
earlier documents describe the earlier delayed design. They do not apply to
the #1100 deployment.

## Findings fixed on this branch

### F-1 (Medium, Solana): a monthly award's reserve could be locked forever

`commit_monthly` reserves the whole month, but awards are committed one leaf at
a time. The `origin` PDA that makes each source commit once is shared with the
owner's direct `commit`. After a month was admitted, the owner could commit a
leaf's source by hand first. That leaf could then never commit, and nothing
released its reserve. The reviewer proved this on a local validator: 20 of
50 USDC-units stayed reserved for nobody, and the owner could not withdraw
them either. A repeated source or an actor ID of zero in an allocation had the
same result.

Fix: `Project.monthly_outstanding` counts admitted gross that has not been
committed. While it is positive, a direct ordinary `commit` fails with
`MonthlyOutstanding`. The new permissionless `release_monthly_award` returns a
leaf to free balance when its source already has an origin or its actor is
zero. A `settled` PDA per month and source, created by both the commit and the
release, lets each leaf settle once; a self-review found that without it a
committed leaf could also be released, which freed another leaf's reserve. Before admission the owner can already turn
the monthly commitment off, so a release of a source consumed before
admission gives the owner no new power. The allocation builder now rejects
repeated sources. The EVM vault commits a month atomically and is not
affected.

### F-2 (Low, Solana; open item L-2 of the 12 October review): the fee leg could stop payouts

`pay` and `withdraw` sent the fee to a fee-recipient token account inside the
same instruction. The program accepts any account that the fee recipient owns,
but the executor and the funding flow always used the associated account. A
mint-authority freeze of that account stopped every payout and withdrawal with
a fee. Fees now accrue in the vault (`Project.accrued_fees`), as on EVM. The new
permissionless `claim_fees` moves them to any fee-recipient token account.
Vault balance checks hold `reserved + accrued_fees`. `readVaultTotals` now
reports `accruedFees` for Solana.

A second, independent review of these patches found that months recorded by
the 316-byte build have no settled markers and are not counted in
`monthly_outstanding`; after `migrate_project` a release could then free a
reserve to the owner. `MonthlyAllocation` now ends with a `released` counter
(100 bytes), so such a month fails closed. Devnet has no such month.

### F-3 (Migration, Solana)

F-1 and F-2 add two u64 fields, so `Project` grows from 316 to 332 bytes.
Devnet already has three 316-byte projects of program
`7E5ZRh5y6Xgg7k3kdS75CMiTE2efoq9PqNWR5fCVDavQ`. The permissionless
`migrate_project` grows such an account and starts both counters at zero.
`config-plan.mjs migrate` builds the unsigned transactions.

## Findings that do not need a code change under the accepted policy

- **Rotation execution block (EVM and Solana).** On the #1081 source, a binding
  made in the block that executes the rotation lost the cancel-cap waiver. With
  immediate activation and zero delays the owner has no cancel window at all,
  so the edge has no effect on the #1100 deployment.
- **Two monthly caps one second apart (EVM and Solana).** Period windows are
  adjacent, so a key can commit the last second of one window and the first
  second of the next. The documentation says "one cap per calendar month"; the
  exact rule is one cap per period window.
- **The adapters do not compare the on-chain project ID with the registry
  project.** The reviewed manifest pins the exact vault or project PDA, and
  admins approve each manifest, so two projects cannot share a vault without a
  reviewed manifest error. The adapter config holds no on-chain project digest
  to compare.
- **The owner can remove single monthly awards before admission (Low).**
  Between the allocation freeze and `commit_monthly`, the owner can commit an
  award's source by hand; after admission that leaf is released to free
  balance. Before admission the owner can already turn the whole month off.
  The attester must check that no origin exists for any source when it signs,
  and submit `commit_monthly` at once.
- **A bad or unpublished Solana allocation blocks the gate (Medium, needs an
  attester fault).** A root whose leaves are lost, whose total differs from its
  tree, or that repeats a source can never settle completely. Its remainder
  stays reserved and the owner's direct `commit` stays refused for that
  project. A deadline release was not added, because it would return admitted
  awards to the owner, which the PRD forbids. The attester is already a
  total-trust key under the accepted policy.
- **A source repeated inside one Solana allocation.** Only one copy can settle,
  so `monthly_outstanding` would stay positive and the owner's direct
  `commit` would stay refused for that project; monthly commitments keep
  working. The allocation builder rejects repeated sources before the
  identity authority signs, so this needs an attester fault.
- **Solana network domain.** The owner chooses the 32-byte network value and
  the program ID is the same on every cluster. A monthly signature can be
  replayed across clusters only if one identity key signs on two clusters for
  the same project PDA and network value. Use separate identity keys per
  cluster.

## Checked and found safe

Both reviewers listed these attack classes as safe on both chains: signer
and owner checks, account and token-program substitution (Token-2022 is
rejected), PDA seeds and bumps, `init_if_needed` reuse, account revival and
type confusion, checked arithmetic with release overflow checks, fee rounding,
the conservation identity, the Ed25519 instruction check, the Merkle sum tree,
EIP-712 domains with low-s and zero-address checks, reentrancy and exact token
deltas, CREATE2 vault keys, the monthly window calendar, and the deploy
scripts. #1100's proxies disable initializers on implementations, initialize
atomically, check `proxiableUUID`, gate `_authorizeUpgrade` on the factory's
upgrade authority, and require acceptance for an upgrade-authority transfer.
